How it works

Three steps. Then you're done forever.

Set it up once, and the only thing you ever do again is walk over and tap.

1📱

Pick your problem apps

Instagram. TikTok. The one you'd rather not name out loud. They go quiet until you say otherwise.

2🐀

Put your rat somewhere far

By the front door. On the kitchen shelf. Up a flight of stairs. Your phone writes a secret key into it — that rat, and no other, is now your key.

3✨

Walk over, tap, get your minutes

Hold your phone against its head. Apps open for 15, 30, 60 minutes — or as long as you asked for. Then they close again.

🧠 In plain terms: the walk is the point. By the time you've crossed the room, the urge has usually filed its own resignation.

What's in the box

Small app. Very few opinions.

🔒

It really blocks

On iPhone, Apple's own Screen Time does the locking. On Android, a block screen appears over the app.

⏳

Timed windows

15, 30, 60 minutes or open-ended. A countdown shows what's left, and you can lock early.

🔑

Your rat only

Every figure carries its own key, paired to your phone. Someone else's rat won't open a thing.

📊

Gentle stats

How often you made the walk, how often the phone won. Seven days, no lectures.

🔔

A warning first

A nudge before your time runs out, so the lock never ambushes you mid-sentence.

🛜

Stays on your phone

No account, no cloud, no tracking. Works in airplane mode. Nobody sees your list — not even us.

⚠️ The honest bit: on iPhone, Apple doesn't let any app slam the door on another one. So Brain Rat hands the locking to Screen Time and acts as the gatekeeper — the rat decides when you're allowed through. On Android it does the blocking itself.

Questions

Things you were going to ask anyway.

When can I actually have it?

A small closed beta comes first, once Android stops misbehaving on the more idiosyncratic handsets. Waitlist people hear before everyone else — that is the entire point of a waitlist, bébé.

What will it cost?

The beta is free. Pricing after that is undecided, and I refuse to invent a number simply to fill a space on a webpage.

Does the rat come with the app?

They are one product. A box contains one rat, chosen at random from Series 01, and the app is free to download once you have it. Pricing for the box is still being worked out.

Is the browser extension the same thing?

Same spirit, different screen — and it already exists. Not a Rat is free on Firefox and Chrome, it holds your scroll for five seconds instead of making you walk anywhere, and it needs no rat, no phone and no account. Think of it as the understudy.

iPhone or Android?

Both at launch. iPhone is further along; Android is in the thick of it. Neither ships until both behave.

Does it work without internet?

Beautifully. Tapping needs nothing but proximity, and Brain Rat needs nothing but you.

Couldn't I just carry the rat around in my pocket?

You could. You could also eat the entire wheel of cheese. Nobody here is going to stop you — but we will both know, and the rat has a face.
